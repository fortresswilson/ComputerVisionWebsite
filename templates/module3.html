04

Convolution Theorem

Let f(x,y) represent the original image and h(x,y) represent the spatial filter. Applying the filter directly in the spatial domain gives:

g(x,y) = f(x,y) * h(x,y)

The symbol * represents two-dimensional convolution.

Fourier Domain

Taking the Fourier transform of the spatial convolution gives:

G(u,v) = F(u,v) H(u,v)

Therefore, convolution in the spatial domain becomes multiplication in the frequency domain.

Returning to the Spatial Domain

The inverse Fourier transform converts the frequency-domain result back into an image:

g(x,y) = F⁻¹ { F(u,v) H(u,v) }

Therefore:

f(x,y) * h(x,y)   ↔   F(u,v) H(u,v)

The experiment above validates this relationship by applying the same averaging filter using both approaches and comparing the resulting pixel values.
